Elementary cellular automata are one-dimensional systems studied by Stephen Wolfram. Each cell's next state is determined by its current state and its two neighbors, encoded in a single rule number (0-255). Despite their simplicity, certain rules (like Rule 30 and Rule 110) produce remarkably complex, even computationally universal, behavior. The initial condition — a single center cell or a random row — dramatically affects the resulting pattern. Each seed changes the random initialization pattern when random init mode is active.
